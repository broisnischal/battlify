New **Hibernate after a while** (Sleep & Power): 0% overnight without paying for it during the day.

A sleeping Apple silicon Mac still draws around 130 mW, nearly all of it memory sitting in self-refresh — about 2% over a night, and nothing running on the machine can reduce it. The only lever is hibernation, which writes memory to disk and cuts that rail. Deep sleep does exactly that, but leaving it on permanently means every lid close costs a multi-second wake, including the ones where you're back in five minutes.

So wait first. Pick 30 minutes, an hour or three, and the Mac sleeps normally until the lid has been shut that long on battery — then it hibernates for the rest of the sleep. Short closes never reach the delay and still wake instantly; a Mac left shut for the night crosses it and stops drawing power. Opening the lid, or plugging in, puts your own Deep sleep setting straight back.

Off by default. If you've already chosen permanent Deep sleep that setting stays yours — this never overrides it.
